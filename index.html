Things I'm interested in:

  • Security and AI for national security. I built AI agents at CrowdStrike, so I think a lot about what happens when attackers and defenders both have them. Does AI ultimately favor offense or defense? How do governments adopt frontier technology fast enough to matter without giving up oversight?
  • Institutions and power. Past a certain scale, institutions stop being organizations and become something closer to the architecture of reality: they define what is legitimate, and eventually what is even thinkable. Why are they so hard to defeat from the outside? Why might the most powerful people inside them be the least free? And what happens when AI and surveillance make them more entrenched still?
  • Technology and progress. I'm broadly a techno-optimist, and I suspect culture and history are mostly downstream of technology. But I worry about what I call AI-accelerated intergenerational stratification: AI could make life better for nearly everyone while making the circumstances of your birth matter more than ever, because its gains compound fastest for people already at the frontier. Historically, being born poor meant starting with fewer opportunities. In an AI economy, it could mean being shut out of the process that creates them. Is the speed of technological progress starting to outpace the ability of disadvantaged people to participate in it? Are we building a permanent underclass, and what would prevent it?
  • Entrepreneurship. Things start slow, then flywheel. I think most success comes from energy, a clear direction, obsession with the problem, and maximizing your surface area for luck.
  • Development economics. Having grown up between DC and Dubai, I'm interested in why some places transform within a generation while others stall, and whether AI will narrow that gap or widen it.
  • Biotech. Computation is starting to speed up biology. Which breakthroughs are closer than people think? Very interested in BCI's and the software that will come with it.
  • Aesthetics. The best art produces profound emotion and surprise, and holds more meaning than any paraphrase can capture. Why has the world lost so much of its exoticism, mystery, and romance? Is beauty a kind of terror? And can you fairly judge a violinist if you can't play the violin yourself?
  • Literature. I keep coming back to Byron and Goethe's Faust: radical individualism, restless striving, "in the beginning was the deed." How do you live as a Byronic or Faustian person today? Why are the greatest characters forces of nature that you hate but follow anyway?
  • Philosophy. Most of what we think is inherited, from language, culture, and choices made by people we'll never meet. I trust lived experience over ivory-tower theory. Also super fascinated by ideas like Riding the Tiger, The Kali Yuga, and the return of Renaissance Men. Nietzsche in particular
  • Religion. How socially constructed is religion, and does the answer change anything about faith? I'm drawn to the old Islamic ideal of tamaʿdad (تَمَعْدَد): hardiness, simplicity, endurance, and comfort without attachment.
  • Books and ideas. Lately I've been reading Hemingway, Gabriel García Márquez, and books on the technologies that are changing the world.

When I'm not reading or building, I'm usually hiking, planning the next trip, or listening to house music and DJ'ing.
